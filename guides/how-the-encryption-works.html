How Albatross keeps your AI chats private

Most AI chat apps send every message to a company's servers. Albatross runs the AI on your own PC, and everything between your devices is encrypted with a key only those devices have. This page explains exactly what that means.

What stays with you

  • The AI itself: models run on your PC's graphics card through Ollama. Cloud AI models are blocked.
  • Your chats and pictures: saved in your browser and on your PC, not on a server.
  • Your encryption key: the second half of your 16-character code. It is never sent anywhere.

The relay server

Your phone usually can't reach your PC directly, because home routers block incoming connections. So both connect out to a small relay server, which passes messages between them. The relay sees that a message goes to your PC, when and how big it is, and your IP address, but it can't read or change the content.

How the encryption works

Each request is sealed with AES-GCM using a key derived from the second half of your code. The PC answers in numbered, sealed pieces, so nothing can be dropped, swapped or added on the way without your device noticing. Because only your devices know the key, the relay just forwards data it cannot open. If you ever share your code by mistake, make a new one in the control center: the old code stops working at once.

Accounts are optional

You can use Albatross without signing in. If you sign in with Google or email, your settings sync between devices; your PC code and power link are only stored encrypted with a passphrase you choose, which nobody can recover for you.

Open source

All of this can be checked: the app, the host and the relay server are public on GitHub. Details for lawyers are in the privacy policy.
