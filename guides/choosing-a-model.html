Choosing an AI model for your graphics card

Local models come in sizes, measured in billions of parameters (the "8b" or "14b" in their names). Bigger models answer better but need more graphics memory (VRAM). The trick is picking the largest model that still fits entirely on your card.

Why graphics memory matters

A model runs fast when all of it fits in your graphics card's memory. If it doesn't, Ollama moves part of it to the normal memory and the processor, and answers get many times slower. As a rough guide for the usual 4-bit versions Ollama downloads, a model needs a bit more than half a gigabyte of VRAM per billion parameters, plus some room for the conversation.

Good picks by card

  • No dedicated GPU or 4 GB: models around 1 to 4 billion parameters, such as gemma3:4b or qwen3:4b. Fine for short answers and summaries.
  • 8 GB: 7 to 8 billion parameters, such as qwen3:8b or llama3.1:8b. A big step up for writing and coding help.
  • 12 GB: up to about 14 billion, such as qwen3:14b. The sweet spot for many people.
  • 16 GB and more: 20 to 32 billion parameter models for the best quality at home.

Thinking models

Some models think before they answer. Albatross shows the thinking live and lets you choose how hard a model should think. More thinking means better answers to hard questions, but a longer wait.

One model at a time

Albatross keeps one model in memory at a time and shows which one is loaded. Switching models takes a few seconds while the new one loads; the button Free GPU memory empties the card when you want it for a game.

Pictures

Image models run through ComfyUI next to Ollama. Albatross makes sure only one heavy model uses the card at once, so a picture and a chat model don't fight over memory.
