How to set up your own private AI on a Windows PC

Albatross turns a Windows PC into your own AI server. The AI models run on your graphics card, your chats stay on your devices, and nothing is sent to an AI company. This guide takes you from nothing to your first answer in about fifteen minutes, most of which is the model download.

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC. An NVIDIA graphics card with 8 GB of memory or more makes answers fast; without one, smaller models still work, just slower.
  • Some free disk space: a typical model is 2 to 10 GB.
  • An internet connection for the downloads. After that, chatting on the PC itself works offline.

1. Install the Windows app

Download the latest Albatross installer from GitHub and run it. It installs for your user only, so it doesn't need admin rights, and puts Albatross in the Start menu and next to the clock.

On the first start, Albatross checks for Ollama, the free engine that runs the AI models. If it's missing, Albatross downloads and installs it by itself. Then it starts the host: the small background part that connects your devices, runs the models and does the work on the PC. The opening screen shows each step as it happens.

2. Download a first model

Open Settings → Installed models. Albatross suggests models that fit common graphics cards. Good first choices:

  • qwen3:14b: very capable, for cards with about 12 GB of memory.
  • qwen3:8b or llama3.1:8b: for 8 GB cards.
  • gemma3:4b: small and quick, for weaker PCs and laptops.

The download runs in the background with a progress bar. Models are stored by Ollama, not inside Albatross, so other Ollama apps can use them too. See choosing a model for your graphics card for more detail.

3. Start chatting

Pick the model at the top of the chat and write your first message. Reasoning models show their thinking live while they work. Every chat can have its own instructions, and you can let a chat search the web when you want current information.

4. Optional: start at boot

Albatross starts with Windows by default and waits quietly in the tray. If you want your phone to reach the PC even before anyone signs in, turn on Start at boot on the Host page. Windows asks once for admin rights and your password, because the host then runs as a background task.
